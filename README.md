## 01. Project Overview

The Blue Bottle Experiment was an experimental chemistry project exploring the potential of a low-cost chemical system for oxygen indication.

Inspired by the conventional Blue Bottle Experiment, the project investigated colour changes associated with methylene blue and explored whether this principle could contribute to a more accessible oxygen-indication approach.

The project used a modified formulation involving glucose, potassium hydroxide (KOH), hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂), and methylene blue.

The investigation also considered the practical challenges of developing affordable alternatives, particularly the potential replacement costs associated with implementation.

## 02. Research Objectives

The main objectives were:

- Explore colour-changing chemical reactions involving methylene blue.
- Investigate the relationship between oxidation-reduction reactions and visible colour changes.
- Explore the potential of a low-cost oxygen-indication system.
- Consider the affordability and practical feasibility of the concept.
- Identify limitations and opportunities for further research.

## 03. Chemical Components

The project involved four reported chemical components:

| Chemical | Formula | General Role |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose | C₆H₁₂O₆ | Reducing agent |
| Potassium Hydroxide | KOH | Provides an alkaline environment |
| Hydrogen Peroxide | H₂O₂ | Additional oxidising component |
| Methylene Blue | C₁₆H₁₈ClN₃S | Redox indicator |

**Important:** The inclusion of hydrogen peroxide makes this a modified formulation rather than the conventional Blue Bottle Experiment. Its specific reaction mechanism and behaviour require independent investigation.

## 04. Scientific Background

The conventional Blue Bottle Experiment demonstrates a reversible oxidation-reduction reaction involving glucose, an alkaline solution, and methylene blue.

Methylene blue can exist in different chemical states associated with different visible colours.

- Its oxidised form appears blue.
- Its reduced form becomes colourless or nearly colourless.
- Exposure to oxygen can restore the blue colour under suitable experimental conditions.

This makes the experiment a useful demonstration of redox chemistry.

However, a visible colour change alone does not establish an accurate measurement of oxygen concentration. Further calibration and independent testing would be necessary before describing the system as a reliable oxygen sensor.

## 08. Challenges and Practical Considerations

Beyond the chemical investigation, the project involved considering its potential real-world feasibility.

Discussions with businesses highlighted concerns surrounding replacement costs and the affordability of implementation.

This raised an important engineering consideration:

**A technically interesting solution is not necessarily a practical solution unless it is affordable, reproducible, and reliable.**

## 09. Research Limitations

Several areas require further investigation:

- Establishing the reaction mechanism of the modified formulation.
- Determining the influence of hydrogen peroxide.
- Recording reaction timings under controlled conditions.
- Investigating the reproducibility of colour changes.
- Establishing whether the colour response specifically correlates with oxygen.
- Developing a reliable calibration method.
- Evaluating long-term affordability and practical feasibility.

The conventional Blue Bottle Experiment provides a scientific starting point, but it does not independently validate the modified formulation as an oxygen sensor.
